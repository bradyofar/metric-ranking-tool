How this works

We convert each metric into a Z-score (a standardised value). This lets you combine different units like percentages and currency into one comparable index.

What's a Z-score?

A Z-score shows how far a value is from the average, measured in standard deviations.

  • Z = 0 means exactly average
  • Z = +1 means one standard deviation above average (better than roughly 84% of entries)
  • Z = -1 means one standard deviation below average
  • Formula: Z = (value - mean) / standard deviation
About weights

Use any positive numbers to show importance (like 1, 2, 3). We normalise them to percentages automatically, so they always add up to 100%.

Why costs are inverted

For cost metrics (CPM, CPC, etc.), lower is better. We multiply their Z-scores by -1 so higher scores always mean better performance.

Understanding sample size and standard deviation

Sample size matters:

  • Small samples (n<10): Results can be unstable. One unusual value has outsized impact.
  • Medium samples (10-30): Z-scores become more reliable but still sensitive to outliers.
  • Large samples (30+): Z-scores are statistically robust and meaningful.

Standard deviation shows spread:

  • Small std dev: Values are clustered together. Rankings may exaggerate minor differences.
  • Large std dev: Values vary widely. Clear differentiation between performers.
  • Zero std dev: All values identical (we set it to 1 to avoid errors).

Rule of thumb: If the z-score spread is less than 1.0, most entities are performing similarly.
